# Our licensing philosophy & commitment to the community

*From a personal project to a company-supported future.*

## A message from the founder

Magic IndexedDB began as my personal project. I wanted a better way to work with browser databases, and I believed other developers should be able to benefit from the work I put into it. I shared it under MIT because I wanted people to use it, build on it, learn from it, and make things I might never have imagined myself.

Over the years, developers have adopted the library, reported issues, contributed improvements, and helped shape it. I'm grateful for that. Version 3 brings together much of the vision I've had for this project for years: code-first schema definitions, a dedicated migration CLI, a language-independent migration engine, authenticated encryption transitions, and recoverable database upgrades that work on decentralized browser clients. It's the culmination of a lot of engineering, research, experimentation, and testing.

The circumstances behind that work have changed. Magic IndexedDB is becoming an important part of Sayou, the independent software company I'm building. I'm now committing company time, engineering effort, and financial resources to it. That investment is helping make the fuller vision of Magic IndexedDB possible.

I could have taken future development private. I could have completed these features exclusively for Sayou and left the existing MIT releases as the last publicly available versions. The community might never have seen the full vision of this project. I don't want to do that. I want other developers to have access to the technology we're building, and I want them to keep benefiting from future improvements.

But I'm also responsible for the business making that investment possible. I don't want to spend company resources building technology for a direct competitor to use against the very business funding it.

That is why we are introducing the Magic IndexedDB Community License (MICL) with the official version 3 distributions.

## Broad freedom, with a defined business boundary

For uses outside the reserved fields and competitive uses defined in the license, our goal is to preserve much of the practical freedom developers have enjoyed under MIT. Build a small side project or a huge commercial application. Sell it, charge subscriptions, keep your application proprietary, modify the library, and redistribute it subject to the license and notice requirements. We do not impose revenue ceilings, per-user fees, royalties, or registration requirements on permitted uses. Your commercial success is not the reason for our licensing change.

Sayou is a configurable case management platform, with vocational services among its specialized capabilities. Our vision extends beyond any single industry. The license reserves certain healthcare, care and human services, case management, funded service-program administration, and associated specialized functions, whether or not we offer every one today. It also addresses material competition with actual Sayou offerings outside those fields. This is a boundary based on relevant use, not a blacklist of companies.

We recognize that not every organization in a related industry is a competitor. An unrelated product or ordinary general-purpose tool is not automatically restricted because its developer serves a hospital or happens to operate in healthcare. The license defines these distinctions. And if an otherwise permitted product becomes competitive solely because Sayou later enters its market, the protections in Section 5.5 preserve the previously permitted use of earlier versions.

## Restricted does not mean unwelcome

If you're building in a reserved field, or think your application might compete with a Sayou offering, please reach out. Requiring permission is not an automatic refusal and does not mean you will be charged. There are many complementary, creative, and valuable applications in related industries. I'd rather understand what you're building than have you assume that the door is closed.

We may grant free written authorization, agree on a paid license, collaborate, or explore a partnership. Every project is different. Contact us through [sayou.io](https://sayou.io).

## Respecting the community and earlier rights

Previously granted MIT rights remain intact, including rights in source or other materials independently distributed under MIT. We are not attempting to take those permissions away. The new license applies to materials distributed under its terms, subject to independently existing rights.

Because MICL contains field-of-use restrictions, we describe MICL-licensed versions as source-available, rather than open source in the OSI sense. I still believe in the value of open-source development and transparent engineering. Developers should be able to inspect the technology they depend on, understand how it works, and participate in making it better.

We welcome bug reports, technical discussions, suggestions, documentation, and contributions. A contributor agreement for copyrightable contributions to the official project helps us maintain and separately license the project while allowing contributors to retain ownership.

## Looking forward

Sayou's use of Magic IndexedDB gives us a direct incentive to keep improving its security, correctness, performance, browser compatibility, tooling, and developer experience. We want the community to benefit from that work. The intention is not to build a licensing server or require an online permission check: the library is designed to remain capable of offline operation.

Thank you to everyone who's used the project, given feedback, or helped improve it. I want to keep sharing what we build. I also want to protect the ability of our company to keep building it.

— The founder of Magic IndexedDB, with the Sayou team
